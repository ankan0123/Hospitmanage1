QUESTIONS & ANSWERS

Frequently Asked Questions on Treatments

Clear medical answers to help you make informed decisions about your fertility care path.

What is the difference between conventional IVF and ICSI?
In conventional IVF, thousands of motile sperm are placed around an egg in a culture dish to fertilize naturally. In ICSI (Intracytoplasmic Sperm Injection), a single selected sperm of optimal morphology is directly injected into the cytoplasm of a mature egg using a high-precision micromanipulator. ICSI is recommended when there is low sperm count, poor motility, high fragmentation, or previous fertilization failure.
How many cycles of IUI should a couple attempt before considering IVF?
Clinical guidelines typically suggest 3 to 4 well-monitored IUI cycles for women under 35 with clear fallopian tubes and adequate ovarian reserve. If conception does not occur after 3-4 cycles, the cumulative success rate of subsequent IUI attempts plateau, making IVF or ICSI a substantially more effective and time-sensitive path.
Is egg freezing safe and what is the optimal age for cryopreservation?
Yes, modern rapid vitrification (flash-freezing) does not form damaging ice crystals, preserving egg cellular structure with a survival rate exceeding 95%. The biologically optimal age for egg preservation is between 28 and 35, when both egg quality and quantity are peak. However, successful vitrification is routinely performed for women up to age 38 after assessing ovarian reserve.
What clinical options exist for severe male factor infertility or zero sperm count?
For men diagnosed with azoospermia (zero sperm in ejaculate), IRM's andrology team specializes in surgical sperm retrieval procedures including TESA (Testicular Sperm Aspiration), PESA, and Micro-TESE. Even small numbers of retrieved viable sperm can be combined with ICSI to achieve healthy embryo creation and successful biological fatherhood.
